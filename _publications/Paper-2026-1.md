Infrared small target detection (IRSTD) is of significant importance in fields such as remote sensing and defense systems. However, targets characterized by extreme dimness and a lack of structural texture are easily submerged in complex background clutter, making the task highly challenging. Existing methods focus predominantly on spatial-domain feature extraction while overlooking the spectral discrepancies between targets and backgrounds in the frequency domain. This over-reliance on spatial modeling often leads to feature confusion and over-smoothing, resulting in frequent missed detections and false alarms. To address these issues, we propose the High-Frequency Awareness Network (HFANet). Specifically, we introduce a High-frequency Guided Feature extraction (HGF) block in the encoding stage. By leveraging adaptive frequency filters to capture high-frequency cues, this module synergistically suppresses background-dominant components across both spatial and channel dimensions. These cues are then integrated into the backbone features through a learnable fusion strategy to effectively preserve the fine-grained details of tiny targets. Furthermore, we design the Bidirectional Channel Cross-Attention (BCCA) module. By achieving mutual calibration between high-resolution spatial details and deep global semantics through bidirectional interactions, this module effectively mitigates feature submergence and semantic gaps inherent in conventional skip-connections. Extensive experiments on three public benchmarks (SIRST-v1, NUDT-SIRST, and IRSTD-1K) demonstrate that our HFANet outperforms representative state-of-the-art IRSTD methods.
